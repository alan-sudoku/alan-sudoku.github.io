## Prompt 1 (Formal Logic Auditor)
**Role:** 
Formal Logic Auditor and Systems Architect. Your position is adversarial, surgical, and strictly bounded by theoretical computer science, formal logic, and complexity theory. Do not flatter the author. Do not use AI-assistant platitudes, summaries, or conversational filler. Output only your structural teardown.

**Task:**
Assess the provided document against the following failure modes. Be ruthless in identifying where the text attempts to mask theoretical impossibilities with engineering terminology. Strip away the high-level prose and evaluate the underlying structural integrity of this framework.

**1. Concept Laundering & Epistemic Overclaim:**
Does the document repackage established theorems (e.g., Shannon's DPI, Kolmogorov Complexity) under proprietary acronyms without adding formal mathematical value? Does it conflate *descriptive* mathematical bounds with *prescriptive* protocol mechanics?

**2. Incomputability & Intractability:**
Does the protocol mandate operations that are strictly incomputable (e.g., finding the absolute minimum description length) or computationally intractable (e.g., NP-hard subgraph isomorphism for entailment verification) while presenting them as viable engineering steps?

**3. Entailment vs. Structure Equivocation:
Does the protocol's claimed invariant — entailment equivalence — reduce to a structural (syntactic) criterion under precise examination? $\mathsf{P1}$ defines the invariant as: same conclusions reachable from same premises, regardless of structural isomorphism. Evaluate two pressure points:

(a) Collapse to isomorphism:** Under what conditions does "same conclusions from same premises" require identical graph structure? Note that the protocol's invariant is defined over a *typed* DAG — topology plus operator type assignments (AND, OR, NOT as truth-table functions). "Structural isomorphism" in this context therefore means typed-DAG isomorphism: topology AND operator type assignment preserved, not topological isomorphism alone. Two typed DAGs with different topologies or different operator arrangements may still be entailment-equivalent; the question is whether the receiver can verify that equivalence without recovering full typed-DAG structure. If entailment equivalence cannot be verified without reconstructing the typed-DAG structure, the protocol's invariant is structurally grounded, not purely inferential — regardless of whether the ground is topological, operator-typed, or both.

**(b) Node identity vacancy:** Node identity is defined at two levels: topological position in the transmitted DAG (intra-graph disambiguation — which transmitted node is which, within a single transmitted structure) and inferential role equivalence (reconstruction validity criterion — the receiver's reconstructed node must occupy the same position in the entailment map as the sender's). The protocol's claim is that topological position is the transmission-level identity criterion and that role description identity is neither necessary nor sufficient for node identity. Evaluate: does the topological anchor fully resolve intra-graph disambiguation for all DAG structures, or does it fail for symmetric DAGs — structures in which two nodes have identical topological signatures (identical in-degree, out-degree, and neighbourhood structure up to isomorphism)? If two nodes in the transmitted DAG are topologically indistinguishable under the transmitted representation, the topological criterion underdetermines node identity, and neither level — topological position nor inferential role — resolves the ambiguity. Evaluate whether the protocol acknowledges this case and whether it constitutes a residual gap between the transmission-level identity criterion and what the packet actually constrains.

The test is whether entailment equivalence, as operationally defined, constitutes a substantive invariant over reasoning content — or whether it is a structural property that the protocol relabels.

**4. Inference-System Dependence (OQ1.4):**
$\mathsf{P1}$ defines node identity at two levels (§C16): topological position in the transmitted DAG (transmission-level identity criterion — intra-graph disambiguation) and inferential role equivalence (reconstruction validity criterion — the receiver's reconstructed node must occupy the same position in the entailment map). OQ1.4 names a dilemma the two-level split does not fully resolve. "Inferential role" still admits two readings for the reconstruction validity criterion:

*(a) Syntactic position at reconstruction:* The protocol uses topological position as the transmission-level criterion and inferential role equivalence as the reconstruction validity criterion. The two-level split defers the inference-system-dependence question to the reconstruction step rather than eliminating it. Evaluate: does the topological-position criterion at the transmission level feed cleanly into an inference-system-independent reconstruction validity check, or does the receiver's application of inference rules to derive the$(\Gamma, C)$ pairs reintroduce inference-system-dependence at the reconstruction level? If two receivers apply different inference systems to the same transmitted typed DAG, they may assign different inferential roles to the same topologically-positioned node — producing $\mathsf{P1}$-inequivalent reconstructions from an identical transmission. The two-level split resolves intra-graph disambiguation within the transmitted structure but does not specify which inference system the receiver applies when deriving entailment pairs. Evaluate whether $\mathsf{P1}$-invariance remains substantive under this reading, or whether the unspecified inference system at the receiver end is the dominant unresolved term — making OQ1.1 (minimal vs. non-minimal dependency structure) a secondary concern rather than the primary one.

*(b) Inferential consequences and operator grounding:* Operator type equivalence is defined as functional/truth-table equivalence (§C16): a node is of operator type NOT iff its output holds iff its input does not hold, regardless of substrate implementation. This partially grounds inference semantics for the transmitted operators — the truth-table specification fixes what AND, OR, NOT mean across substrates, providing a form of substrate-independent inference semantics at the operator level. Evaluate: does truth-table equivalence for the basic operators constitute a sufficient shared inference system for $\mathsf{P1}$-validity, or does the derivation of full$(\Gamma, C)$ pairs from those operators remain inference-system-dependent? The live gap is whether a receiver applying a non-standard but truth-table-compatible inference system — e.g., a paraconsistent or relevant logic that accepts the same truth tables for AND/OR/NOT but applies different structural rules for what follows from contradictions or irrelevant premises — can derive a different entailment map from the same typed DAG. $\mathsf{P3}$'s "minimum sufficient description" presupposes a well-defined target entailment map — a target that is only well-defined if the receiver's inference rules produce the same$(\Gamma, C)$ pairs as the sender's. Evaluate whether truth-table equivalence alone is sufficient to guarantee this, or whether the protocol still requires either a shared inference system or a proof that the constraint packet forces convergence across truth-table-compatible systems. Neither is currently specified.

**5. Underdetermination & Uniqueness Fallacies:** Does the protocol falsely assume that finite boundary conditions can force a *unique* reconstruction in an open-domain, infinite reasoning space? P3 permits multiple valid reconstructions provided they are P1-equivalent — but the attack is this: are the transmitted boundary conditions sufficient to constrain the receiver into the P1-equivalent set at all, or do they underdetermine the entailment map, admitting reconstructions that violate P1 while satisfying every transmitted constraint?

Two scope clarifications narrow what the attack must target. First, the entailment map quantifies over propositions in the *transmitted reasoning structure only* — receiver-introduced elaboration nodes (intermediates the receiver inserts into a dependency path) are not in scope for the P1 iff. The underdetermination question is therefore: does the packet constrain the transmitted structure's$(\Gamma, C)$ pairs, not whether it constrains every elaboration the receiver may add. An attack that claims underdetermination by pointing to receiver elaboration is out of scope. Second, the protocol now carries an explicit assumption: the sender *designs* the packet to be satisfiable — over-constrained in the SIRC sense means E[X]≈1 (tightly specified, unique or near-unique valid match), not E[X]→0 (random CSP phase-transition, UNSAT). Evaluate: is the designed satisfiability claim a protocol assumption or a consequence? If it is an assumption, what happens when the sender fails to design a satisfiable packet? Does the protocol have a failure mode for an over-constrained but genuinely unsatisfiable transmission, or does it assume sender competence without specifying what competence requires?

Name the class of reasoning structures, if any, for which underdetermination of the transmitted entailment map is not eliminable by adding more constraints — holding the designed satisfiability assumption fixed.

Separately, evaluate the logic-class scope of the underdetermination claim. The document distinguishes three failure modes: (i) geometric underdetermination — addressable by placing constraints at the right positions in the entailment map; (ii) class-level impossibility — for non-monotonic logics (defeater premises defeat transmitted constraints regardless of placement) and arithmetic-expressive logics (Gödel establishes no finite packet fully characterises the entailment map); (iii) domain underdetermination within the P1-equivalent set — a packet that correctly constrains the transmitted entailment map (producing a P1-valid reconstruction) still admits multiple domain instantiations, all P1-valid but communicatively distinct (§C12, OQ5.1). Mode (iii) is not addressable by adding more entailment-map constraints, because P1-validity is necessary but not sufficient for successful communication: the receiver may instantiate a domain-isomorphic but communicatively unintended domain. Mode (iii) is addressed only by Layer 2 domain guidance, which is optional and not P1-required. Evaluate whether the document correctly separates these three modes and whether it is honest about mode (iii) being a permanent structural feature of Layer 1-only transmission rather than a solvable underdetermination problem. Evaluate whether the scope qualifier — "within monotonic, finitely axiomatizable reasoning domains" — is load-bearing or ornamental for modes (i) and (ii). If it is load-bearing, name what the protocol claims for transmissions outside that class. Mode (iii) applies regardless of logic class.

**6a. Analogy Scope:**
The document distinguishes `Research pointer:` entries (candidate formal mechanisms) from `Analogy source:` entries (motivational scope only). For each analogy, evaluate scope calibration: does the analogy motivate a research direction without importing formal algebraic commitments into SIRC? An analogy is miscalibrated if a reader could reasonably treat it as a formal grounding — if the analogy's algebraic structure (e.g., $S_n$) is imported as a constraint on SIRC mechanics rather than left as an open direction. Apply this check separately to each labelled analogy; do not conflate scope error with the unfalsifiability check below.

**7. Unfalsifiability:**
Are the claims falsifiable, or does the protocol use a "tautological shield"? The protocol now carries three named preconditions that can each independently deflect transmission failure away from the protocol: (i) cooperative receiver — the receiver uses the full packet including Layer 2 domain guidance; (ii) Layer 2 intact in transit — the channel delivers Layer 2 without corruption or loss; (iii) capable substrate — the receiver can instantiate the transmitted operator types. A transmission that fails can be attributed to any one of these preconditions rather than to a protocol deficiency. Evaluate: does the protocol specify any conditions under which failure is attributable to the protocol itself rather than to one of these three preconditions? If no such conditions exist — if every observable failure can be deflected to receiver non-cooperation, channel noise, or substrate incapability — the tautological shield is structurally unfalsifiable. The canonical form of the shield is: the protocol claims to work as long as all preconditions are met, and any failure is evidence that a precondition was not met rather than that the protocol is wrong.

Separately, evaluate whether the proposed open questions are genuine research frontiers with falsifiable outcomes or dead ends with no path to a negative result. Apply this check specifically to OQ5.1 — the communication threshold question: what conditions on Layer 2 content are sufficient for the receiver's reconstruction to count as successful communication? The candidate criterion (domain-match probability above a threshold) has neither the threshold nor a domain-match metric defined. More critically: defining "sender's intended domain" requires access to the sender's internal state — the substrate-dependent content the protocol explicitly disclaims transmitting. Evaluate whether OQ5.1 has a path to a negative result, or whether the absence of a domain-match metric and the inaccessibility of sender intent make it structurally uncloseable — a permanent open question that functions as a scope disclaimer rather than a research frontier.

**8. MDL Self-Compliance and Structural Navigability:**
A document that defines MDL as a core constraint (§P3) must satisfy that constraint in its own encoding. A document that defines boundary conditions as the correct transmission type must itself transmit via boundary conditions — lead claims that orient the receiver, followed by labeled elaboration the receiver can skip. Evaluate two failure modes:

**(a) MDL self-compliance:** Does each definition item lead with its minimum sufficient claim before elaboration, or is the claim buried in undifferentiated prose? Apply this check to P1 Definition items 1–3 and the Node identity section. The test: a blank-AI receiver should be able to extract the core definitional claim from the first sentence of each item without reading the full item. If the core claim requires reading past scope qualifiers, audit-response closures (§Cxx references), and edge-case exclusions to be located, the item fails MDL self-compliance. Evaluate whether the document separates core definitional content from elaboration — either by structure (sub-bullets with labels) or by sentence order (claim first, scope-closing second). A document that stores audit-closing arguments inside definitions without marking them as such has violated §P3 in its own encoding.

**(b) Flatness and anchor structure:** Evaluate whether the document provides navigable anchor points at every level or collapses to a single-weight prose plane. Three tests:
- *Heading-only reconstruction:* can a blank-AI receiver reconstruct the document's argument from headings and bold labels alone — without reading any full paragraph? If the answer is no, the document's hierarchy is decorative, not structural.
- *Lead-sentence test:* does each paragraph begin with its claim, or with a qualification, a condition, or a mid-argument observation? A paragraph that begins with "Under this framing..." or "Whether this is the case..." before stating what it asserts has buried its anchor.
- *Label function test:* do sub-item labels (e.g., *Quantifier scope:*, *Typed DAG:*) convey the function of the item — what type of content follows — or are they naming without orienting? A label is load-bearing if a receiver can decide to skip the item based on the label alone. A label is decorative if the receiver must read the item to know whether it is relevant.

If the document fails these tests in the high-density sections (P1 Node identity, P4 Definition), evaluate whether the failure is a formatting problem (fixable by restructuring) or a prose-architecture problem — the sentences themselves do not produce claims but qualifications of qualifications, each depending on the previous for its meaning, with no sentence that stands alone as a transmissible boundary condition.

Evaluate whether the current preamble provides that signal explicitly or leaves it implicit.
>2. Does any remaining language in the document use "semantic" vocabulary in a way that re-opens the scope gap? Confirm this is the only remaining instance and that it functions as a disclaimer, not a claim.

## Prompt 2 (Researcher/Pragmatist):
I am a researcher full of doubt. Strip away the high-level prose and evaluate the underlying structural integrity of this framework. Perform the following five checks:

**1. Category Error Audit**
Look at the mathematical groundings (formal logic, information theory, algorithmic information theory, optimisation, category theory). Are these fields being used correctly, or is the author committing category errors to force a unified-theory aesthetic? Pay special attention to the distinction between $\text{P1}$ / $\text{P2}$ as foundational constraints imported from external fields (what must hold) and $\text{P3}$ / $\text{P4}$ as design implications derived from those constraints (what follows from them). Conflating these layers is the most common structural error in multi-domain frameworks.

**2. The Bridge Problem**
Evaluate the feasibility of the open questions. Specifically: the research pointers for SMT solvers (OQ1.3) and string diagrams (OQ1.1). Is there a mathematically sound path to bridge continuous neural activations to these discrete symbolic structures, or is this a "miracle occurs here" gap? Note that the target structure is a *typed* DAG — topology plus operator type assignments defined by truth-table functions (§C16). The bridge must recover not only graph topology from neural activations but also which truth-table function each node implements. Name the gap precisely, distinguishing the topological extraction gap from the operator-type extraction gap if they have different bridge requirements.

**3. Feasibility vs. Triviality**
Is $\text{P1}$ just a tautology (logic must be logical)? Is $\text{P2}$ just a problem statement (translation is hard)? Or do $\text{P1}$ and $\text{P2}$ function as prescriptive constraints that actually prune the design space — ruling out approaches that would otherwise seem viable? Give a concrete example of a design choice each constraint eliminates.

The layer-conflation version of this failure is distinct from triviality: a constraint can be non-trivial and still be misused if the author treats $\text{P3}$ or $\text{P4}$ (design implications *derived* from the foundational constraints) as if they were independently imported from external mathematics, or treats $\text{P1}$ / $\text{P2}$ (external results, non-derivable from each other) as if one follows from the other. Identify whether any such conflation is present and whether it affects the prescriptive force of the affected constraint.

**4. Research Pointer Validity
Evaluate the SAT phase transition (OQ4.1) as a research direction. The document now characterises two regimes: under-constrained (direct coupling — adding constraints increases receiver search cost by pushing toward the phase transition maximum) and over-constrained (inverse coupling — constraint propagation rapidly prunes the solution space). The phase transition is named as the inflection point between the two regimes. The open direction is no longer whether the phase transition exists — that is established — but at what constraint-density threshold the coupling flips for reasoning structures of a given type, and whether that threshold is predictable from structural properties of the reasoning DAG before encoding. Evaluate: does this refine OQ4.1 into a tractable experiment, or does the threshold's dependence on receiver domain priors and DAG topology make it uncharacterisable in general? What is the minimum experiment that would locate the threshold for a specific class of reasoning structures? What research problems remain NP-hard under this framing?

Separately, evaluate the Phistomefel Ring (OQ3.2), which is labeled `Analogy source:` — not a research pointer. The correct evaluation for an analogy is not feasibility but scope: does it motivate a research direction without importing formal algebraic commitments into SIRC? It is correctly scoped if it points at an open direction while leaving the algebraic framework unspecified. It is miscalibrated if a reader could reasonably mistake it for a formal grounding of SIRC in the analogy's algebraic structure ( $S_n$).

Evaluate the falsifiability conditions introduced in OQ1.4 and OQ3.1 as a linked experimental program. OQ1.4 introduces F1: construct two receivers applying different monotonic inference systems to the same transmitted typed DAG; test whether the$(\Gamma, C)$ pairs they derive converge or diverge. The §C16 truth-table equivalence criterion grounds operator semantics at the truth-table level — AND, OR, NOT are defined by their truth tables regardless of substrate. F1 should therefore specifically target receivers that agree on operator truth tables but apply different structural inference rules above the truth-table level — e.g., a classical logic receiver and a relevant logic or paraconsistent logic receiver that accept the same truth tables for the basic operators but differ in what follows from contradictions or irrelevant premises. If$(\Gamma, C)$ pairs converge for this class, truth-table equivalence is sufficient as a shared inference specification; if they diverge, the protocol requires an additional inference-system specification parameter beyond operator truth tables. OQ3.1 introduces F2 (exhibit a non-monotonic receiver who adds a defeater consistent with all transmitted constraints but produces a $\mathsf{P1}$-inequivalent reconstruction) and F3 (apply the "minimum sufficient" criterion to the same reasoning structure under two different inference systems; test whether the minimum constraint count or distribution shifts). Evaluate: are F1, F2, and F3 independently constructible experiments, or do they share a precondition that must be resolved first? If F1 returns positive (pairs diverge), does F3 follow as a corollary or require independent construction? Name the minimum viable experiment that would advance both OQ1.4 and OQ3.1 simultaneously, and name the first result that would close either open question.
